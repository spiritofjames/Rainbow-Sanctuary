What brings this gathering together

A deep journey.
A human place to land.

There is the song in the ceremony. And there is the conversation the next morning, the quiet walk, the person who listens while you find your words.

This gathering brings plant medicine, living ceremonial traditions, spiritual practice, and the work of integration into one shared week. It is an invitation to turn toward your life with honesty—not to become someone else, but to meet yourself more fully.

01 · Living traditions

Depth carried through relationship.

Hanan brings years of learning and initiation with maestros, shamans, and curanderos. His plant-medicine and musical practice meets Stephanie’s spiritual and energy-awareness work, with James contributing emotional-intelligence practice and integration. Different paths come together without losing what makes each distinct.

02 · Music that meets the moment

Not a fixed soundtrack.

Hanan describes his ícaros as responsive to what is unfolding. Guests have spoken of songs shared with the whole circle and moments of singing directed to them personally.

03 · Life between ceremonies

The insight matters. So does what follows.

Shared meals, nature, rest, and honest conversation give the experience somewhere to land. Integration asks a practical question: what might change in the way you listen, relate, make choices, or care for yourself when you return home?

A personal journey, not an experience to match. Some participants described closeness and clarity; others also met intensity, discomfort, or uncertainty. There is no particular vision, emotion, or breakthrough you need to have, and no promise that every question will be resolved.

What might bring you here

Space for what
you are carrying.

Perhaps your surroundings no longer fit, an old pattern keeps returning, or you simply want to find your people. You do not need a perfect intention—or a dramatic story—to begin a conversation.

  • Outgrowing your surroundings

    Your old rhythm or environment may no longer feel like yours, while a new way is still taking shape. A week away can give you room to question what you have inherited and explore a wider perspective on how you want to live.

  • Looking for your people

    You may long for open-minded company and conversation that goes deeper than the surface. This small group offers a chance to meet others with curiosity and care, without needing everyone to share the same beliefs or experience.

  • When emotions take the wheel

    Stress, fear, or familiar reactions can leave you feeling less in charge of how you relate. Alongside reflection, we practise noticing feelings, pausing, and choosing a response you can carry into daily life.

  • Patterns that are hard to leave

    You may be questioning a habit, dependence, or relationship—perhaps what you understand as a karmic entanglement. We can listen without judgment and discuss whether this setting is suitable; the retreat is not a substitute for addiction care or other professional support.

  • Uncertain what comes next

    A chapter may be ending before the next one is clear. Quiet, conversation, and time away from the usual noise may help you explore your questions; clarity can unfold gradually, and no particular answer is promised.

  • Seeking deeper renewal

    You might feel a need to attend to your body, emotions, and spiritual life together. Rest, embodied practice, and integration create space to explore what you are carrying, without claiming that one week will heal or purify everything.

Curious and cautious can exist together.

Ask about preparation, personal limits, and the support available before deciding whether this retreat is right for you. The week includes emotional-awareness practice and reflection; integration is about applying what matters in ordinary life, not chasing a single breakthrough.

Where the experience becomes practice

Not more ceremonies.
More meaning in daily life.

A profound moment can open a door. Integration is the patient work of bringing what matters through that door and into the life you actually live.

Stephanie and James bring emotional awareness, reflection, and grounded daily practice into this part of the retreat. Together, we explore how to recognise emotions, stay present with them, and choose a response with greater care. The aim is not to rush a transformation or chase the next experience.

  1. 01

    Notice what is here

    Give feelings and impressions room before trying to explain everything. What stayed with you? What still feels uncertain? Not every image needs to become a literal instruction.

  2. 02

    Practise a different response

    Explore the pause between feeling and acting. Listening, naming a need, holding a boundary, or speaking with more honesty can make an insight tangible.

  3. 03

    Choose what to carry home

    Identify a small, realistic practice, conversation, or change you want to try. Consider the support you need and leave space to revisit what you understood over time.

Integration is individual and ongoing. The December programme will set out the planned sessions; any support after the retreat, its scope, and any separate cost will be confirmed before commitment.

James’s story · My first ayahuasca retreat

I waited five years.
I wanted to trust the people guiding me.

My first ayahuasca retreat was here in October. For five years, I had opportunities to take part—even invitations that would have cost me nothing—but I kept waiting. I wanted to feel a real trust in the people who would guide me, and in the space we would enter together.

I arrived during a difficult period in my life. What unfolded felt, to me, like a profound remembering. Receiving a sacred name touched me so deeply that I felt it through my whole body. I experienced a closeness to the plants and to the sacred that is difficult to put into words.

A song came from that journey. For me, it was a way to give expression to something I had experienced in ceremony—to let it become part of my life, rather than only a memory.

“It is the most powerful experience of my life, and this is just the beginning.”

And that is why integration matters so much to me. I do not want to measure a journey only by what happened in ceremony. I want to ask how I listen, how I relate, how I meet my emotions, and how I live what I have understood. That is part of the work I now bring alongside Stephanie.

This is my experience and my spiritual interpretation—not a prediction of yours. If you are curious and cautious at the same time, I understand. You can ask questions, take your time, and decide what is right for you.

Questions people ask before choosing

Clear answers, before you decide.

Your questions are part of discernment—not an obstacle to enrollment. Start with what matters most to you.

Can I enquire if I have never attended a plant-medicine ceremony?

Yes. First-time participants are invited to begin with questions and a private fit conversation. Previous experience is not assumed, and an enquiry does not commit you to attend.

What might a plant-medicine ceremony feel like?

Ceremonial plant-medicine practices can bring profound shifts in perception, emotion, and physical experience. Responses vary and can be intense or unpredictable. The team should explain the format, known risks, and available support clearly before you decide.

Is this retreat intended to cure trauma, depression, anxiety, or a health condition?

No. The retreat is a spiritual and experiential gathering, not medical treatment or psychotherapy, and no specific outcome is promised. Continue appropriate professional care and discuss any relevant history during confidential suitability screening.

Is this retreat right for me if I already have years of inner-work experience?

It may be. The week is designed for both sincere first-time participants and experienced practitioners who want dedicated space for reflection, embodied practice, ceremony, and integration. Fit is considered individually rather than by spiritual vocabulary or status.

What if I feel nervous or uncertain?

Uncertainty is a valid reason to slow down and ask more questions. The application process exists so you can understand the experience, share concerns privately, and decide without pressure. Not proceeding is also a responsible decision.

How is suitability considered?

Relevant physical health, medication, mental-health history, pregnancy status, previous ceremonial experience, support needs, and other contraindications can affect suitability. Answer screening questions honestly and never stop or change prescribed medication in order to attend.

Can I attend but decline a particular practice?

Informed consent and personal agency matter. You should be able to ask what is essential, what is optional, what alternatives exist, and how support is provided if you pause or decline. These details are confirmed before acceptance.

When and where is the retreat?

The next retreat runs from 3–9 December 2026 for seven days and six nights. Venue and arrival details are shared privately after enquiry and confirmation.

What is the price?

The December retreat investment is USD 3,500 per person. Confirmed inclusions, the payment schedule, cancellation terms, and the authorised payment route are provided in writing before you make any payment.

What is included in the week?

The December plan is a seven-day, six-night residential retreat built around preparation, ceremonial work, shared reflection, rest, and integration guidance. The final schedule, accommodation arrangements, meal plan, specific practices, and follow-up details are confirmed in writing before payment. Descriptions of October experiences are not a fixed December itinerary.

What support is provided before and after?

Preparation and integration guidance are part of the retreat. Accepted participants receive the confirmed schedule, preparation requirements, facilitator contact route, and follow-up details in writing.

When should I arrange flights?

Only after written acceptance, final venue confirmation, payment and cancellation terms, legal-readiness confirmation, and explicit travel instructions.

Can I request a discovery call before deciding?

Yes. Choose Request a discovery call and share your questions. The team will contact you to arrange a suitable time. This requests a conversation; it does not instantly book a call, reserve a retreat place, or create a payment obligation.

How are dates and availability confirmed?

The Events calendar shows public dates. Cohorts or private availability that are not yet published are confirmed directly after an enquiry. Please do not arrange travel until you receive written confirmation.

What happens after I enquire?

You provide your email and WhatsApp number, describe what you are looking for, and accept the relevant policies. The team reviews the request and follows up with fit, availability, next steps, and any missing commercial details.

Where can I review cancellations, privacy, and wellbeing boundaries?

The footer links to the Terms, Privacy Policy, Wellbeing Disclaimer, and Children & Family Safeguarding Policy. These become part of the consent step when you submit an enquiry.
