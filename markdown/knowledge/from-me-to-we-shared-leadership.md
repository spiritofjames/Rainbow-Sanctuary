# From me to we: a practice of shared leadership

A reflection on moving from individual control toward transparent, cross-cultural, and mutually supportive forms of leadership.

**Written by**

Stephanie Wu, Author

**Reviewed by**

Rainbow Sanctuary Editorial Team, Website editorial team

**Updated**

2026-11-04

## Strength does not have to become control

People who care deeply about a project often know how to act decisively, carry responsibility, and solve difficult problems. Those capacities matter. They can also become isolating when one person feels they must carry everything, when urgency makes listening seem inefficient, or when confidence becomes a substitute for collaboration.

The movement from “me” to “we” is not a request to become passive. It is an invitation to let individual strength become more useful to the whole.

## Begin with a transparent question

Before an important decision, pause to ask: what is this choice serving? Is it responding to the shared purpose, or protecting my own comfort, role, fear, or preferred outcome? The answer may not always be obvious, which is why it helps to make room for respectful challenge and reflection.

Transparency is not public self-exposure. It is the practice of naming relevant assumptions, conflicts, limits, and interests so that other people can understand the decision in front of them.

## Make difference an asset

Cross-cultural work brings different communication habits, histories, expectations, and ways of showing care. A group does not need to erase those differences to act together. It needs agreements that allow people to speak clearly, listen carefully, and repair when impact differs from intent.

One person’s blind spot may be another person’s lived expertise. Shared leadership works best when contribution is invited from more than the loudest or most established voice.

## Practise a wider kind of leadership

A useful leader creates conditions in which others can contribute responsibly. That may mean sharing information, clarifying decisions, asking who has not yet been heard, or stepping back so another person’s gift can become visible.

The goal is not to remove responsibility. It is to distribute attention, capability, and care so that the work is less dependent on any one person.
