One vision, different kinds of work

Rainbow Sanctuary is the community-facing field of practice; the School of the Universe is the learning part of that vision; and the Planetary Symbiosis Network, or PSN, is its wider framework for connecting people, projects and places. They are related, but they are not interchangeable names for a single finished service. This guide is for people arriving for the first time and wondering where they might fit.

In brief: begin with the work that interests you, distinguish present activities from future ambitions, and ask for the details of a particular invitation before making a commitment. Shared language is a starting point for conversation, not a requirement to agree with every spiritual or philosophical idea.

What Rainbow Sanctuary means in practice

Rainbow Sanctuary describes an evolving ecosystem concerned with inner growth, relationships, learning, co-creation and care for the Earth. A person might encounter it through a gathering, a learning activity, a private session, a retreat enquiry or an offer to contribute practical skills. These are different relationships, with different questions and responsibilities. Joining a conversation about one does not enrol someone in the others.

The current homepage presents the purpose, core team, places, offers and needs together. Its spiritual language expresses the team’s worldview. Statements about wholeness, Source or an original blueprint should be understood in that context, not as a medical assessment, a scientific finding or a promise about what will happen to a participant.

Where the School of the Universe fits

The School is described as the educational heart of the work, with Awakening the Original Code as its core course. The public vision connects online learning, practice across distance and in-person deepening. The School platform is preparing for beta; that description should not be read as a promise that every proposed course or function is already available. Ask which specific learning opportunity is open now.

Young people and adults have distinct needs and appropriate practices. Existing children and family activities have their own published pathway and guardian-facing information. They should not be assumed to be identical to the future School platform. A family can explore those activities without taking part in adult retreats, private spiritual work or the wider network’s development.

What the Planetary Symbiosis Network adds

PSN expresses the larger ambition: people and places exchanging knowledge, care and useful work rather than trying to build everything in isolation. A local learning project may have experience another community needs. A land-based initiative may need operational support. A contributor may bring a skill while also needing mentoring, a clear role or realistic limits on their time.

The network’s value has to be demonstrated through those actual relationships. Twelve core sanctuaries and 144 light points are future goals in the public vision, not a verified count of operating locations. Likewise, the three place descriptions on the homepage are owner-reported status summaries, not evidence of access rights, legal arrangements, accommodation availability or an invitation to arrive without agreement.

Conscious technology is an emerging strand

The public page also describes SI / Conscious AI: a proposed guide for understanding knowledge and finding opportunities to work together. Design foundations are described as being in place, while the guide itself is still to be built. Explorations involving higher-dimensional intelligence belong to the project’s spiritual and philosophical inquiry. They do not establish a working technology or a verified route to universal knowledge.

This distinction matters when deciding how to contribute. A software engineer can discuss privacy, accessible interfaces or knowledge organization without being asked to accept a metaphysical claim. A spiritual practitioner can share a perspective without presenting it as technical evidence. The existing article on evidence, exploration and symbolic language explains this boundary more fully.

A simple way to choose your next step

Use this orientation checklist before sending an introduction:

  • Name the area that interests you: learning, community practice, a place, a project or technology.
  • Say whether you are exploring an existing activity or proposing something new.
  • Explain what you bring and what support you would need.
  • Ask who currently holds that part of the work and what is actually ready.
  • Keep payment, travel, residency, safeguarding and data questions attached to the particular activity, not to the vision in general.

A useful message might be: “I am an educator exploring family learning. I would like to understand what is open now, how guardians participate, and whether there is a suitable way to offer feedback.” It gives the team a clear starting point without implying agreement to a course or collaboration.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to join all parts of the ecosystem?

No. You can ask about one activity or contribution without joining the whole vision. Learning, adult retreat participation, community collaboration and technical development are different pathways. Clarify the scope of the particular invitation, including any requirements, before deciding whether it suits you.

Is the School already a fully available platform?

The current public description says the School platform is preparing for beta. That is different from saying every proposed learning pathway is open. Check the published family information for existing activities and ask the team about the status, format and requirements of any specific School opportunity.

Does a network vision mean I can visit a place now?

No. A place appearing in a vision or progress summary is not an open invitation, a booking confirmation or a residential offer. Ask the responsible local team about current access, purpose, capacity and practical arrangements. Do not book travel on the basis of a general description alone.

Start with one clear question

Rainbow Sanctuary, the School and PSN become easier to understand when each is connected to a real activity and an honest stage of development. You do not need to solve the whole picture before making contact. Read the area that matters to you, then use the co-creation invitation to begin a specific conversation.
