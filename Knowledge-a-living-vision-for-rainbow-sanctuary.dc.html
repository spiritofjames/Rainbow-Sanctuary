A living vision for Rainbow Sanctuary

Rainbow Sanctuary is an evolving place for learning, relationship, contribution, and care for the living world.

A place to practise relationship

Rainbow Sanctuary is a living experiment in how people might learn, contribute, and care for one another while remaining connected to place and the wider living world. It is not a finished blueprint or a promise that one model can solve every difficulty. It is an invitation to practise a more attentive way of being together.

The Sanctuary holds a simple orientation: human wellbeing, community life, education, ecology, and practical responsibility belong in conversation with one another. When any one of these is separated from the rest, it is easy to lose sight of the relationships that make a place viable.

Stewardship begins close to home

For us, care for the Earth begins with concrete choices: how we treat land, water, resources, neighbours, time, and one another. It asks for humility about what we know, respect for local conditions and laws, and willingness to learn from people whose experience differs from our own.

Stewardship is not a claim of mastery over nature. It is a practice of participation. It can include restoring what has been depleted, reducing unnecessary harm, sharing knowledge responsibly, and making decisions with future generations in mind.

A vision that remains open to learning

The language of vision can be poetic because it helps a community name what it hopes to protect. At Rainbow Sanctuary, that language points toward cooperation, regeneration, and a wider sense of belonging. It should never replace evidence, practical planning, accountability, or care for people’s real circumstances.

We therefore treat this vision as a direction rather than a guarantee. We learn by acting carefully, observing what happens, listening to feedback, and adjusting our approach.

An invitation to contribute with care

People come with different gifts: practical skills, questions, cultural knowledge, time, attention, creativity, and experience of caring for others. No single contribution is the whole work. A resilient community is built through many forms of participation and through clear agreements about responsibility.

If this vision resonates with you, begin with a grounded question: what kind of relationship—with yourself, with others, or with the living world—would you like to practise more carefully?
