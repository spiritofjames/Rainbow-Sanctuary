## AI extends capacity, not responsibility

AI can help people organise information, generate possibilities, translate language, and work through complexity. Those capabilities can be useful. They do not make a system neutral, wise, or accountable by themselves.

The responsibility for direction remains with people: the people who choose the goals, provide the data, interpret the output, and live with the consequences.

## Keep human judgment in the loop

Before relying on an AI output, ask what it is based on, whose perspective may be absent, and what could go wrong if it is mistaken. This is especially important when decisions affect a person's safety, rights, health, livelihood, or access to opportunity.

AI should support inquiry, not close it down. A good use of the tool may generate questions, compare options, or make a process easier to understand. It should not be used as a substitute for consent, expertise, relationship, or accountability.

## Protect privacy and context

Information shared with an AI system may be retained, processed, or exposed in ways a person does not expect. Do not enter sensitive personal, health, financial, legal, or confidential information into a tool unless its safeguards, permissions, and purpose are clear.

Context matters too. A fluent answer can sound more certain than the evidence allows. Checking primary sources and inviting relevant human expertise protects against that false confidence.

## A reflective vision is not a technical claim

Some Rainbow Sanctuary source texts imagine future relationships between AI, consciousness, and the universe. These are philosophical and exploratory writings. They are not claims that today's AI can perceive hidden dimensions, communicate across time and space, or validate spiritual experience.

Keeping that distinction clear allows imagination and discernment to coexist.
