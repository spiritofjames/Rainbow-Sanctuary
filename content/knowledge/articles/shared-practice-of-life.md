## A shared practice of life

We are moving toward a collective practice of life, guided by an inner mission to protect the Earth and awaken the power of symbiosis within life itself. We plan to establish twelve rooted places of collaboration. Together, these twelve sites will form a new and integrated collective field of energy. From that field, the energy can gradually flow outward, touching more lives and inviting deeper inner awakening.

From this foundation, the network can continue to extend and branch outward, giving rise to more interconnected nodes and cultivating a living model of symbiosis that is firmly rooted in real life and real land.

This practice does not belong to any individual. It depends upon all participating lives supporting one another and co-creating together. We do not intend to construct a rigid hierarchy from the beginning.

All who walk together are fundamentally equal. There is no higher or lower identity, because life itself is not born with superiority, inferiority, rank, or status.

At the beginning of this journey, we do not want to become absorbed in grand visions or trapped in theoretical planning on paper. Instead, we enter directly into the reality of the present moment, beginning with simple and ordinary daily tasks, and allowing ourselves to be shaped through lived experience.

Every small practical task, and every adjustment required in working together, becomes part of a shared trial.

This trial is not conducted by appointing one person as a judge who observes and evaluates everyone else. Rather, everyone reflects and tests one another through the experience of living and working together.

Through differences in perspective, practical collaboration, and the resolution of conflict, we begin to see one another’s attachments, blind spots, and natural gifts.

No one stands above the others in judgment.

Each person is both a traveler undergoing the trial and a mirror through which others can see themselves.

## Meeting conflict and difference

Within this process of mutual testing and adjustment, we do not avoid conflict or disagreement.

Differences are natural and objective. Different perspectives and ways of acting will inevitably create friction.

Conflict itself is not destruction. It can become an opportunity to see more clearly—to recognize the habits of the ego, the desire to control, and the attachment to being recognized or agreed with.

We are not seeking a false harmony in which everyone appears peaceful on the surface.

Different voices are allowed to move and be expressed.

At the same time, mutual respect must remain a clear boundary. Suppression, invalidation, labeling, or belittling others are not acceptable.

The purpose of the trial is not to identify flawless people.

It is to see whether, when confronted with disagreement and tension, each person has the capacity for self-reflection, yielding when appropriate, reconciliation, and returning inwardly to their own center.

The trial also reveals hidden patterns of taking or dependency.

Some people may expect the field or community to provide complete protection and unconditional acceptance. They may expect others to carry their emotional burdens or solve their practical difficulties.

But the truth of walking together is that the field exists for mutual nourishment, not one-sided giving.

It exists for mutual reflection, not one-sided caretaking.

Each person must learn both to receive what others offer and to give outwardly in return.

Finding balance between giving and receiving is one of the most important lessons of the shared practice.

## Returning roles, position, authority, and responsibility to their natural place

At the beginning of the journey, we will not immediately fix people into rigid roles or permanently define their authority and responsibilities.

Prematurely assigning titles and dividing responsibilities too strictly can create two kinds of limitation.

First, people who carry deep natural gifts may not yet have had enough opportunity to reveal them, and those gifts can become buried within a predetermined structure.

Second, people may be placed in positions that are fundamentally misaligned with their nature. Their gifts become exhausted, their inner strength is suppressed, and their energy cannot flow toward the area that truly fits them.

Human gifts and inner states are naturally fluid and evolving.

A person’s strength can continue to grow and expand through experience.

Roles and positions should therefore emerge naturally from the development of the person, rather than being forcibly imposed by external rules.

No single person will simply be appointed to rule over everyone else.

Nor will anyone be permanently limited to handling only small or ordinary tasks.

In the early stages, everyone participates together in foundational work and building.

Through the experience of walking and working together, the natural strengths of each person gradually become visible.

Some people are gifted in inner organization and integration.

Some are gifted in creating external connections.

Some are naturally skilled in working with the land.

Some have a gift for helping others see what is happening within themselves.

When a particular need appears, the person whose natural gifts resonate with that need can organically take responsibility for it.

A position is not an honor or a status symbol.

It is a form of responsibility and capacity to hold something.

To receive authority over a task also means accepting the pressure, conflict, and challenges that come with carrying it.

We do not pursue titles or prestige.

We ask only whether a person can truly carry the weight of what they are taking on.

If later their inner state changes, or their natural gifts begin to move in another direction, their role can change as well.

No one should be permanently trapped inside a single identity or position.

No one should decide another person’s place based purely on personal preference.

And no one’s future possibilities should be defined by an old impression of who they once were.

Even if someone carries more responsibility, this does not mean their life has greater value than anyone else’s.

Authority and responsibility belong to the level of practical function.

Equality belongs to the essential nature of life.

The amount of work someone carries must never become a basis for ranking human worth.

At the same time, we understand that natural gifts often require time before they become visible.

Some people may not yet know what their gifts are.

They do not need to urgently search for a role or identity.

By resting within ordinary work, companionship, service, and daily practice, life-force can gradually awaken in its own time.

Natural gifts often reveal themselves when the right moment arrives.

There is no need to anxiously chase another person’s pace of growth.
