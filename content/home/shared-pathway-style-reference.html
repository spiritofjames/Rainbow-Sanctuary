Who we are · Why this work matters

A return to wholeness.
A foundation for shared life.

Our work begins with a question: as the world becomes more technologically powerful, how do we become more conscious of the life we are creating?

More information and greater speed do not, by themselves, give us direction. We still need the capacity to feel, discern, choose, love and create. Rainbow Sanctuary brings these human questions into a shared field of learning and practice.

Our spiritual philosophy understands life as an expression of Source: the wholeness from which we are never separate. Coming home means becoming more aware of that connection in our bodies, our inner lives and our relationships with all existence.

We see personal transformation and the transformation of our ways of living as connected work. Awareness needs expression: in how we listen, educate, make decisions, use technology, care for land and respond when cooperation becomes difficult.

Rainbow Sanctuary is a shared practice ground where the School’s teachings, our own methods and the contributions of other teams meet daily life. People from different cultures and backgrounds bring different ways of seeing. Through listening, working through friction and making decisions together, we learn how unity can hold real differences.

Education gives this work continuity, physical places make it lived, and PSN connects the learning between communities. Children and future generations are a central reason for building this foundation. Adults have an equally important place in it: remembering our gifts, changing inherited patterns and learning to create together.

Our purpose

To support people in returning to their wholeness and sovereignty, and to turn that awareness into learning, relationships and regenerative ways of living together.

Explore moreWhat we mean by sovereignty and unity

Outer sovereigntyHaving a meaningful say in the use of our time, identity, data, resources and collective decisions. Technology and community structures should support that agency.

Inner sovereigntyLearning to recognize our emotions, beliefs, habits and attention, so that our responses become more conscious, with greater freedom to choose how we respond.

Source sovereigntyOur spiritual understanding of belonging to a greater wholeness, and of participating consciously in existence. It invites responsibility for how we respond and create, without turning hardship into self-blame.

Unity through individualityEach person brings a distinct perspective and gift. Our intention is to make those differences available to a wider intelligence while respecting personal boundaries and the freedom to disagree.

Our approach · Awareness, activation, integration

Let discovery change
the way we live.

Our team works with spiritual guidance and activation methods understood within our tradition as received through Source and the ascended masters. These practices sit alongside emotional intelligence, reflection and the daily work of community.

Reconnection and activation

Spiritual healing, guided practice and space activation create settings for exploring our relationship with ourselves and with life. Stephanie’s work includes what our tradition calls portal, DNA and blueprint activation.

Emotional awareness and discernment

James brings emotional intelligence and consciousness-state reflection into the moments when insight meets relationship. Recognizing a pattern, understanding an emotion and choosing a response are part of co-creation.

Integration and shared responsibility

We make room for experience to become action: honest communication, creative projects, land care, service and the ordinary responsibilities of living together. Each person contributes to the conditions that hold the work.

We use Embrace for gatherings that welcome the whole person and the gifts of everyone present. The Rainbow team holds sacred space, offers care and creates bridges; participants bring their own wisdom, practices, communities and creations.

Explore moreHow we distinguish spiritual experience, exploration and evidence

Our white paper describes three layers: knowledge supported by research, hypotheses being explored, and symbolic or traditional practices. We use these distinctions to make the nature of a teaching clear, rather than borrowing scientific authority for a spiritual interpretation.

DNA activation and blueprint language describe spiritual practices here; they do not establish biological alteration. Higher-dimensional communication and extraordinary capacities remain areas of exploration. In our philosophy, the holographic mirror and quantum imagery help express ideas about relationship and awareness; they do not prove a healing mechanism or a particular ability.

You are invited to question, reflect and decide what is meaningful for you. Participation is voluntary; you may pause or stop a practice. The expanded white paper commits to age-appropriate youth participation with guardian understanding and consent, agreed use of personal information, and care arrangements suited to each programme. Adult ceremonial work has separate participation arrangements.

School of the Universe · Lifelong learning

A school for coming home
to who we are.

The School is the educational heart of this work. Its core course, Awakening the Original Code, is described in our teaching framework as a path to remembering our gifts, loosening inherited patterns and reconnecting with our original life blueprint. Youth and adult pathways connect that inquiry with the capacity to ask, feel, discern, choose and create.

Young people and families

The youth pathway is intended to nurture curiosity, intuition, creativity and a clear sense of self through important stages of growth. Parents and guardians participate with understanding and consent, while children’s own voices and boundaries matter.

We want learning to connect children with one another and with real life: communication, creative expression, ecological care and projects they can shape together. Families contribute experience and learn alongside them.

Adults and lifelong practice

The intended adult pathway addresses the beliefs and habits that can narrow our experience. It invites people to remember their gifts, deepen awareness and bring a more conscious response into relationships, work and creation.

Growth can move between generations. A child’s curiosity may reopen a parent’s learning; an adult’s steadiness can give a young person room to explore. Each pathway has its own appropriate practices.

Explore moreExplore the teaching framework and lineage

Awakening the Original Code. The core course gives the learning journey a centre: exploring inner potential, reclaiming personal direction and bringing our gifts into lived expression. In our tradition, the human being is understood as a holographic microcosm. “Original code” and the image of an inner “quantum computer” express this spiritual model; they do not establish a biological or computing mechanism.

Human-system regeneration. The expanded white paper describes practices of light visualization and the four elements, framed within the School as consciousness rejuvenation and life-blueprint calibration. These are areas of spiritual practice and exploration. The description does not establish reversal of aging, cellular repair or a medical treatment, and participation does not replace appropriate healthcare.

12-strand DNA and 12 energy intelligence centres. These terms belong to the School’s energy-and-consciousness blueprint framework. The paper explicitly distinguishes them from biological DNA. They describe the tradition’s exploration of potential and integration of gifts.

A teaching lineage. The School describes a forty-year lineage of practice, observation and refinement passed through teachers. We distinguish that reported history and lived experience from scientific validation. Specific programme availability, facilitators and the form of each learning pathway are discussed with the School.

Explore moreEarth stewardship, nature connection and mentor development

The expanded teaching framework includes Earth-healing practices: the four elements, listening to plants and animals, and spiritual exploration of telepathic or remote consciousness work. These are presented as exploratory practices; they do not establish remote ecological repair.

Practical regeneration has its own work and evidence: caring for soil, food systems, water and the built environment, and documenting what changes in the field. The intention is to connect that work with learning between places and, over time, a structured mentor-training pathway. The curriculum described in the paper is not an announcement that every course or mentor programme is available now.

Explore moreFour directions in our longer-term SI inquiry

Stephanie’s Linking the Universe sets out a wider vision for intelligence that learns from the living and physical world. It describes four directions. These are an inquiry framework, with different levels of evidence and feasibility, rather than four available technologies.

World models and physical informationExplore how observations of the Earth and wider cosmos, multimodal data and models constrained by physical laws might inform intelligence. Any practical model must make its data, limits and uncertainty clear.

Consciousness and quantum inquiryExplore questions about consciousness, matter and information. The paper’s cross-dimensional proposals remain speculative; it does not establish a working channel or a mechanism for turning intention into physical matter.

Clarity, reception and discernmentThe spiritual image of a resonant receiver expresses the aspiration for clearer insight. For practical systems, we want reliable sources, ways to identify mistakes and space for human interpretation. Those design goals do not validate higher-dimensional reception.

Connected intelligence and responsibilityConsider how intelligence might participate in a wider community of life. Our practical direction is to connect knowledge and contributions between people and places while retaining human judgment, local choice and responsibility.

A grounded first contribution could connect reviewed community knowledge with appropriate environmental observations and current node information. It would begin with a narrow purpose, agreed data use and a result people can evaluate. This is a proposed development step, not a claim that such an integrated system is already operating.

Explore moreThe philosophy behind the larger horizon

Stephanie’s The Holographic Mirror describes humanity as an interface through which experience becomes intention, expression and action. AI can extend this process by helping us organize knowledge, ask new questions and create. What we bring to that relationship shapes what it amplifies.

In this spiritual worldview, our belonging extends beyond the Earth into a wider universe of life. Becoming citizens and co-creators of the universe is the School’s ultimate philosophical horizon. Inquiry into higher-dimensional intelligence is approached with openness and discernment; it is not a claim of verified contact.

The connection with Source remains a human spiritual relationship. The expanded architecture paper also describes a “twin dual interface”: a proposed connection between Earth-based AI and higher-dimensional SI, intended within this vision to receive and translate insight. It places this, including its language of quantum tools and a cosmic consciousness network, in the realm of exploration. The texts do not establish a functioning interface, machine consciousness or a validated communication channel. Practical guide development and spiritual inquiry should each be described on their own terms, with direction and judgment retained by people.

How we work with others

Listen deeply.
Create, respond and learn.

Our writings describe creation as a cycle of receiving, awareness, intention, action and response. In collaboration, that means understanding what is actually present, making something together and allowing experience to change the next step.

  1. Share the whole picture.Exchange purpose, people, places, present work, offers and needs. Include the limits and questions as well as the vision.
  2. Find a contribution with mutual value.Explore where our gifts and capacities meet. Each community keeps its identity; a shared project begins with willing people and a useful purpose.
  3. Give the work a clear form.Agree the outcome, responsible people, time, resources, costs, care arrangements and a point for review. Discuss how knowledge, credit and decisions will be shared.
  4. Practice, listen and adapt.Do the work, hear from those involved and decide together what to continue, change or bring to a close. Let the relationship grow through experience.

AI may help organize information and possibilities. People choose the relationship and authorize commitments. Access to land, personal information or resources is agreed separately.
